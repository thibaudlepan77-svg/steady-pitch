Songs for a bass voice

22 songs whose melody stays inside E2 to E4 from start to finish, out of 432 measured. Allow three semitones of transposition either way and it becomes 75.

That is a small number, and it is the honest answer to why basses run out of things to sing. Popular music is written around the middle of a tenor, so a melody that stays inside E2 to E4 from beginning to end is genuinely rare. Allowing three semitones of transposition roughly triples the list, which is what most basses end up doing anyway.

Why a voice type is a bad way to pick songs

A voice type is not a range. It is where your voice sits happily and where it changes gear, and two people called bass can have their break in different places. A choir director put it to me more bluntly than that, and he was right. Using E2 to E4 as a filter is a starting point, not a verdict.

If you would rather work from your own two notes, the range test measures them in about a minute in the browser, then hands them straight to the full song list.

Where these numbers come from

They are read out of MIDI transcriptions from the Lakh MIDI Dataset, CC BY 4.0, by Colin Raffel. Human beings typed those melodies in by ear and about a fifth of the songs transcribed twice have versions that disagree by a whole octave. Only songs with two agreeing transcriptions and a range a voice can produce made it here, and some bad octaves still got through. The main page names one it still gets wrong.
